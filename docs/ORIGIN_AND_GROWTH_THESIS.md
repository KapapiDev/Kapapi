# KAPAPI Origin and Growth Thesis

Status: **canonical product/business thesis**  
Updated: 2026-09-03

## 1. Founder-origin problem

KAPAPI did not begin from the abstract goal of building another professional-talent marketplace.

The original founder problem was simpler:

> **I have spare time after work and useful office skills. I want to pick a small piece of work I can actually finish, complete it, and earn money without becoming a full-time freelancer, building a storefront, or taking a fixed-shift part-time job.**

The mirrored client problem is:

> **I have work that needs to be finished, but it is too irregular, urgent or disproportionate to justify hiring another employee or starting a heavyweight vendor-selection process.**

KAPAPI connects:

- a person's available time + usable skill
- an organization's unresolved work + insufficient current capacity

The supply mechanism is therefore **task-first**, not profile-first or service-listing-first. The client buys an execution contract from the start: **deliverable + price + completion time + revision boundary + recovery boundary**. KAPAPI handles the market, assignment, QA and recovery internally, then returns a result for client acceptance or revision (D-033.1–.12, D-035).

### Boundary clarification

The founder-origin word **“small”** describes the first unmet behavior. It does not define the permanent market by price, skill level or transaction size.

The durable product boundary is:

> **bounded work that can be scoped, completed and accepted as a clear transaction.**

That may be a simple office-support task or higher-value skilled professional-support work. KAPAPI should start where transactions are easiest to prove, then expand upward as trust, liquidity and execution capability grow.

---

## 2. Core product principle: work first

> **KAPAPI is not where a freelancer primarily creates a service storefront and waits to be discovered. It starts as a place where work already exists, a worker can choose work they can finish, complete it and get paid.**

Worker mental model:

`see real work → judge fit → propose price + completion time → selected/assigned → finish → get paid → build verified history`

Client mental model:

`work request / files → KAPAPI structures SOW → approve the Execution Contract → KAPAPI executes / checks / recovers → receive result → accept/revise`

Preferred client-side line:

> **사람을 찾지 말고, 할 일을 올린다.**

The core object is the work itself, not a freelancer listing.

---

## 3. Initial jobs to be done

### Worker

A worker does not need to think:

> “I want to become a freelancer.”

A valid early motivation is:

> **“I have one or two free hours. Is there work here that matches what I can do?”**

Early worker value:

- no fixed shift
- no need to invent a service listing first
- no need to build an audience
- no requirement to commit to a long project
- visible result and deadline
- explicit price and completion-time commitment
- verified completions compound into future trust

### Client

The client does not necessarily need a new professional relationship. The client needs a result.

Typical trigger:

> **“Someone needs to finish this, but hiring, searching, explaining and managing a vendor feels disproportionate to the work.”**

Capacity Gap / Skill Gap / Deadline Gap / Employment Gap remain useful demand mechanisms, not the founder-origin story itself.

---

## 4. Early work shape

The strongest initial work unit is digitally transferable, bounded and has a visible deliverable.

### Data & Documents

- spreadsheet cleanup / comparison tables
- document formatting and structured data entry
- research/data organization
- PPT cleanup

### Content & Production

- image cleanup/editing
- product listing work
- translation/proofreading
- subtitle/cut editing

### Skilled Support

- defined CAD/drawing support
- rendering
- small web/code fixes
- other bounded office/professional support tasks

Good early properties:

- clear input
- clear output
- hours to roughly two days initially
- remote/digital delivery
- mistakes are revisable
- low irreversible liability
- client can inspect the result
- enough comparable supply can exist
- enough value exists to support viable transaction economics

KAPAPI should not optimize for the smallest or cheapest possible work. The strategic goal is a **clear work unit**, not a cheap labor unit.

---

## 5. Why price × completion time belongs at the center

The founder-origin problem implies fragmented availability. Workers have different amounts of spare time and clients have different deadlines and willingness to pay.

Every proposal therefore carries:

- **price**
- **committed completion time**

This allows spare capacity to become economically legible.

A worker with a free evening can compete on speed. Another can compete on price with a longer commitment. Urgent work makes completion time especially valuable.

This mechanism connects **available human capacity** to **time-sensitive unresolved work**. It is visible on the worker surface and may be explained in `이용 방법`. KAPAPI uses the price × completion-time signal internally to build quotes, allocate execution and arrange backup capacity; the client approves the resulting contract.

---

## 6. Relationship to recommendation and routing

Task-first describes the worker market. The default client flow never requires studying or comparing worker profiles or proposals.

```text
Worker side
work discovery → price + completion-time proposal → execution → verified history

Client side
work request → structured SOW → 실행 계약 승인 → KAPAPI internal execution / QA / recovery → result → accept/revise
```

The original side-job insight is the **supply/market bootstrap**. Result-oriented UX is the initial client experience. What develops over time is KAPAPI's ability to deliver it with more accurate quotes, reliable routing, lower operating effort and effective recovery.

## 7. Growth ladder

### Phase A — Bounded online work market

Prove that people will pick finishable online work and clients will approve execution contracts for it. Early KAPAPI may use a concierge back office for scoping, assisted quotes, procurement, assignment, QA and recovery.

Learn what gets posted, what attracts supply, price/completion-time distributions, contract approval, completion/revision behavior, operator minutes, where specifications fail and which transaction-value bands support completed-outcome contribution after execution, payment, QA/support, revision and recovery costs.

### Phase B — Skilled and professional work

Expand into work where relevant career and domain context materially improve execution. Add stronger task-specific trust, career verification and category-liquidity measurement.

### Phase C — Repeat business capacity

Once clients repeatedly submit work, KAPAPI becomes more than a place to find side jobs.

Add repeat-work templates, preferred/verified worker pools, existing vendor import, organization history, reusable specifications, routing intelligence and backup/replacement capacity.

### Phase D — Outcome / Execution Layer

Target:

`work in → scoped execution contract approval → KAPAPI executes / checks / recovers → result out → accept/revise`

The client already receives this result-oriented flow in Phase A. Phase D expands its reliability, range and economics. Depending on category evidence, internal execution may involve human workers, AI, deterministic automation, specialist partners or hybrid/multi-worker workflows. Near-instant quotes and stronger outcome commitments require repeatable scopes, predictable costs and recovery capacity.

## 8. Strategic narrative

```text
Founder cannot find the kind of side work he wants
↓
Build a market where work exists first
↓
People turn spare time + skill into completed work
↓
Organizations approve result + price + completion time + revision/recovery boundaries
↓
KAPAPI executes internally and accumulates scope, quote, price, time, QA and recovery data
↓
Internal quoting, routing and recovery become increasingly reliable
↓
Repeat businesses use KAPAPI as external capacity
↓
Human + AI + automation + partners expand execution capacity
↓
KAPAPI becomes work-execution infrastructure
```

Worker-side line:

> **할 수 있는 일을 골라서, 끝내고, 돈 번다.**

Client-side line:

> **사람을 찾지 말고, 할 일을 올린다.**

North Star:

> **해야 할 일을 올리면, 결과로 돌아온다.**

## 9. Founder-problem fit for 2026 모두의 창업

1. The founder personally searched for after-work side income.
2. Existing options did not match the desired behavior: choose bounded online office/professional work, finish it, get paid.
3. That observation leads to a task-first market rather than service-storefront-first discovery.
4. The opposite side is organizations with bounded work that does not justify permanent staffing or heavyweight outsourcing.
5. The prototype shows clients approving an execution contract, KAPAPI handling execution internally and returning a result for acceptance/revision; the worker side shows task discovery and price + completion-time proposals.
6. Architecture/CAD provides a founder-domain testbed for higher-skill work, but KAPAPI is not limited to construction/architecture.
7. The “small task” origin is an entry point, not a low-value microtask market definition.

## 10. Validation implications

### Worker questions

- Will people choose bounded online work for side income?
- Which task sizes and payments create response?
- Does price × completion time produce meaningful trade-offs?
- Can KAPAPI attract useful supply without storefront-building?

### Client questions

- Will clients submit work currently done personally, delayed, assigned internally or sent to known vendors?
- Which work is bounded enough to delegate but valuable enough to pay for?
- Will clients approve a defined execution contract without shopping for workers?
- Do clients understand the deliverable, price, completion time and revision/recovery boundaries?
- Does repeat work emerge?

### Market/economics questions

- time to first valid proposal
- eligible proposals per task
- assignment rate
- completion rate
- on-time rate
- revision rate
- client acceptance
- worker effective earnings
- operator minutes per completed result
- support/recovery cost
- completed-outcome contribution margin
- minimum viable transaction-value band by category
- repeat client rate
- repeat worker rate

The strongest proof is **real work moving from unresolved to completed through KAPAPI at viable economics**.

---

## 11. Product guardrails

KAPAPI should not drift into:

- generic expert directory
- portfolio-first social network
- storefront clone
- cheap clickwork/microtask marketplace
- long-project IT outsourcing board
- heavy offline staffing agency
- universal regulated-professional marketplace before category evidence exists
- a product that requires users to learn fictional/game terminology

Every major feature should make it easier for workers to find/complete suitable work, make it easier for clients to turn unresolved work into a result, or materially improve trust/liquidity/routing/execution/recovery.

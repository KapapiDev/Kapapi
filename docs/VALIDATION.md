# KAPAPI Validation

Updated: 2026-09-03

KAPAPI is **not market-validated yet**.

This document separates observed evidence, working hypotheses and experiments. Do not convert founder intuition, interviews, prototype behavior or subsidized usage into market proof.

---

## 1. Primary validation question

### Supply

> **Will people with spare time + useful skills choose bounded online work, commit price + completion time, complete it and return for more work?**

### Demand

> **Will clients externalize bounded work, pay for accepted results and return with more work?**

### Market

> **Can those behaviors meet with enough category-specific liquidity, trust and economics to create repeat transactions?**

### Economic band

> **Which kinds of work have enough transaction value to support worker earnings, payment, support, revision, recovery, acquisition and eventual KAPAPI contribution?**

The strongest proof is not signup count.

> **real external client + real work + real worker + real completion + accepted result + real payment**

Stronger still:

> **both sides return without subsidy.**

---

## 2. Founder-origin supply hypotheses

Founder-origin behavior:

> **“퇴근 후 남는 시간에 내가 할 수 있는 작은 온라인 업무를 골라 끝내고 돈 벌고 싶다. 프리랜서 상점을 만들거나 고정 알바를 잡고 싶은 건 아니다.”**

Hypotheses:

- S-01: people want task-level side income without creating a storefront
- S-02: bounded digital work is easier to evaluate than open-ended freelance projects
- S-03: price + completion time lets people monetize fragmented availability
- S-04: visible real work is a stronger activation trigger than “create your seller profile”
- S-05: task-specific history compounds into better future opportunities
- S-06: repeat participation depends on effective earnings, clear scope and fair client behavior

The word “small” does not imply a permanent low-price market. The product hypothesis is about **finishable work units**.

---

## 3. Client hypotheses

Mirrored demand problem:

> **“누군가 끝내야 하지만 채용이나 복잡한 외주 절차를 밟기에는 애매한 일이 있다.”**

Hypotheses:

- D-01: capacity/deadline/skill/employment gaps create bounded external-work demand
- D-02: self-completion/overtime is a major competitor
- D-03: posting/explanation/selection friction suppresses outsourcing
- D-04: price + completion time creates meaningful trade-offs, especially under urgency
- D-05: relevant career/task history lowers delegation anxiety
- D-06: existing vendor relationships do not remove all need for new/backup supply
- D-07: repeat work can move KAPAPI from discovery into external capacity
- D-08: recommendation/routing can eventually reduce decision burden
- D-09: each work category has separate liquidity
- D-10: a viable band exists between “do it myself/AI” and “large project procurement”

---

## 4. Observed founder-domain evidence

The initial architecture/CAD interview remains useful as hypothesis formation, not market proof.

Observed themes:

- overlapping projects created temporary capacity pressure
- straightforward work was still completed personally because delegation felt bothersome
- under real deadline pressure, willingness to pay rose materially
- relevant career signaled lower explanation burden
- confidentiality/NDA affected willingness to send files
- trusted vendors may be reused directly, creating disintermediation risk
- new supply remains useful when existing vendor availability/quality/specialty changes
- the strongest desired client outcome was ultimately “put the work in and inspect the finished result”

Do not generalize one founder-domain interview to all SMEs or categories.

---

## 5. Price × completion-time validation

Test whether price + committed completion time changes real choices rather than merely looking differentiated in a prototype.

Measure:

- distribution of proposed prices
- distribution of completion commitments
- relationship between urgency and willingness to pay
- whether faster commitments win a premium
- whether lowest price actually wins
- whether task-specific trust outweighs small price differences
- whether workers change proposals according to their available time
- whether clients remember and understand the mechanism after a short demo

Kill/rethink the mechanism if completion time adds little information or increases confusion without improving allocation.

---

## 6. Transaction-value / anti-microtask experiment

For each candidate category observe:

- transaction value
- worker effective earnings
- payment cost
- acquisition cost where measurable
- clarification/support minutes
- revision/rework minutes
- dispute/recovery allowance
- KAPAPI staff/AI operating cost
- contribution after direct transaction costs

Test whether categories need:

- a minimum payment
- minimum bundle size
- task bundling
- different fee structure
- exclusion because economics are structurally weak

Desired discovery:

> **the minimum viable transaction-value band for each category micro-market.**

---

## 7. Task-first discovery experiment

Compare:

### A. Task-first

`open work → choose suitable work → submit proposal`

### B. Storefront/profile-first control where practical

`create seller/service profile → wait/search for leads`

Measure browse→proposal conversion, time to first proposal, abandonment, willingness to return, effective earnings and worker preference.

---

## 8. Client posting / scope experiment

Compare manual posting with KAPAPI-assisted posting where the client uploads files + rough instruction and KAPAPI structures the work and asks only missing questions.

Measure:

- minutes to valid work specification
- follow-up questions
- corrections before posting
- worker clarification requests
- revisions caused by ambiguity
- client preference

---

## 9. Selection experiment: recommendation before auto-routing

Compare:

### Mode A — lightweight client choice

Client sees a compact set of eligible proposals.

### Mode B — KAPAPI recommendation + client confirmation

KAPAPI highlights one recommendation with visible rationale; alternatives remain available.

### Mode C — concierge/manual routing

Used only in controlled experiments where helpful.

Measure decision time, recommendation acceptance, override rate, client confidence/regret, completion/on-time/revision outcomes and client management minutes.

Preferred prototype default is **Mode B**.

---

## 10. Founder-funded supply experiment

Founder-originated real work may seed supply and exercise the transaction engine. Architecture/CAD is useful because the founder can originate realistic tasks and inspect results.

Measure qualified worker acquisition, proposal density, price/completion-time distributions, completion/on-time/revision, willingness to return and effective earnings.

Rule:

> **Founder-funded work validates supply and transaction mechanics, not external client demand.**

---

## 11. External paid client experiment

Strong evidence:

> **external client spends own money on an accepted result.**

Stronger:

> **same client returns with another self-funded task.**

If first-use subsidies are used, measure the self-funded second transaction rather than celebrating subsidized usage.

---

## 12. Category strategy

The public prototype may show:

- Data & Documents
- Content & Production
- Skilled Support

Actual transaction experiments should normally open **one or two micro-markets at a time**.

For each category track paid demand, qualified active supply, eligible proposals, response time, price/completion distributions, completion/on-time/revision/dispute rates, backup supply, repeat behavior, support/recovery cost and transaction contribution.

---

## 13. Quality / recovery experiment

Initial quality architecture:

1. objective automated preflight where reliable
2. client acceptance/revision
3. platform dispute/recovery only where needed

Measure automatable checks, client inspection time, revision/dispute rate, staff minutes per failure, replacement success and recovery cost.

---

## 14. AI / automation execution experiments

For suitable categories compare:

- human worker only
- AI/automation only
- AI first pass + human verification
- human execution + automated preflight

Measure cost to completion, turnaround, error/revision rate, client acceptance, human review minutes and recovery cost.

---

## 15. Disintermediation / retention

Test whether users stay for contracts/NDA, payment/admin evidence, work/vendor history, recommendation, availability, backup supply, replacement/recovery, reusable templates, organization workflow and eventual outcome assurance.

Do not assume hiding contact details solves retention.

---

## 16. Metrics that matter

### Supply
- qualified active workers per category
- browse → valid proposal conversion
- time to first valid proposal
- repeat worker rate
- effective worker earnings

### Demand
- external paid work count
- self-funded repeat client rate
- client repeat frequency
- transaction value
- client management time

### Marketplace
- eligible proposals per task
- assignment/selection rate
- price / completion-time distributions
- category liquidity

### Execution
- completion rate
- on-time rate
- acceptance rate
- revision/rework rate
- failure/dispute rate
- replacement/recovery rate and cost

### Economics
- payment cost / transaction value
- support minutes per task
- revision/recovery cost per task
- acquisition cost where measurable
- worker effective earnings
- contribution after direct transaction costs
- minimum viable transaction-value band by category

Vanity when isolated:

- total signups
- pageviews
- total worker count across unrelated categories
- raw task count without transaction value or completion quality

---

## 17. Go / Pivot / Kill

### GO

Evidence repeatedly shows useful workers choose and complete bounded work, clients externalize and pay for it, price + completion time creates useful allocation information, trust is sufficient, economics survive revision/recovery and both sides repeat.

### PIVOT

Demand exists but only certain categories or price bands work, low-value work needs bundling/floors, clients want different selection control, existing-partner management is stronger than open-market discovery, or AI/automation handles some categories better.

### KILL / major rethink

Repeated real tests show supply does not participate at viable economics, clients still prefer self-completion, scope/coordination cost erases value, quality/recovery cost is structurally too high, price + completion time adds little value, most target work is either trivially automated or too complex for bounded transactions, or the market collapses toward low-value tasks with no viable economics.

Do not protect the idea from negative evidence.

# KAPAPI Validation

Updated: 2026-09-01

This document separates **observed evidence**, **working hypotheses** and **future experiments**.

KAPAPI is **not market-validated yet**.

---

## 1. Primary business question

The dominant early risk is not PLAYER acquisition.

It is:

> **Will external GMs repeatedly submit real professional work and pay to have it completed through KAPAPI?**

A marketplace with abundant PLAYERs and weak GM/QUEST demand fails.

The strongest evidence is not signup, interview enthusiasm or subsidized usage.

> **external GM + own money + real QUEST + repeat use**

---

## 2. Initial GM interview — architecture

Source: founder acting as an architecture GM, 2026-09-01.

This is a single qualitative interview. It is useful for hypothesis formation, not market proof.

### Observed situation

- Multiple projects overlapped.
- A new project required converting a hand-drawn/current-condition building drawing into CAD.
- The work was straightforward for a competent CAD user.
- Because other projects overlapped, the GM wanted someone else to do it.
- In reality the GM completed it personally, taking roughly two days.

### Why the GM did not outsource

- Explaining/delegating felt bothersome.
- The work was easy enough that “I will just get it done” felt faster.
- The GM also attempted to solve the task through an AI-skill approach before doing it manually.

### Conditions that would trigger outsourcing

The GM said outsourcing becomes attractive when:

- even working late may not meet the deadline
- a more important design task would be sacrificed
- 3–4 projects overlap
- outsourcing price is acceptable
- a capable person can start quickly
- the PLAYER can understand the task with little explanation

### Actual fallback behavior

In an extreme scenario where the task must be done but there is no time, the GM said the likely current behavior is **working overnight**.

This supports the hypothesis that KAPAPI competes with owner overtime/self-completion, not only other marketplaces.

## 3. Willingness-to-pay / PRICE × DELIVERY signal

For an approximately 8-hour CAD-like task under pressure:

- natural willingness: roughly KRW 100k–200k
- stated maximum under urgency: roughly KRW 300k

Hypothetical choice:

- A: KRW 100k, next morning, rating 4.8, 42 completions
- B: KRW 150k, 23:00 today, rating 4.9, 87 completions
- C: KRW 220k, 19:00 today, rating 4.9, 156 completions

The GM chose **C**.

Reasoning:

1. looked most competent/trustworthy
2. fastest
3. largest completion history
4. the result could be inspected after dinner and the GM could leave work

### Interpretation

The GM was not buying cheap CAD labor alone.

The GM was buying:

- deadline certainty
- confidence in output
- preserved capacity for higher-value work
- reduced overtime

This is qualitative support for **price × delivery-time choice**, not proof that every category behaves this way.

---

## 4. Trust/security finding

The GM was reluctant to send project materials to an unknown external worker because architecture projects can contain confidential client/project data.

Willingness increased when the hypothetical platform included:

- NDA/confidentiality agreement
- clear non-disclosure obligations
- project-data handling restrictions
- remedies/liability for leakage
- documented contractual safeguards

### Relevant career is a trust shortcut

A profile such as:

> “5 years at an architecture office”

was highly meaningful because the GM inferred:

> “I can probably hand over the source drawing without explaining every professional convention.”

Relevant career is therefore a proxy for **lower delegation/management cost**, not only prestige.

## 5. Repeat trade and vendor-network finding

The GM's company used the same CG vendor for roughly ten years.

That relationship began as ad-hoc outsourced work, then grew into a long-term vendor relationship through successful work and referrals.

More recently the company tested a new vendor because of quality concerns. The new vendor had much better quality, so the company now uses:

- newer vendor for quality-sensitive work
- older vendor for urgent/simple work

### Interpretation

Professional outsourcing behaves like a **vendor portfolio**, not necessarily one permanent winner.

A new KAPAPI PLAYER can become a long-term external partner when:

- the preferred vendor is unavailable
- quality declines
- a different specialty is needed
- comparison is useful

This creates discovery value, but also a major disintermediation risk.

---

## 6. Disintermediation finding

When a realistic direct-deal scenario was presented with a PLAYER who had already performed well, the GM said they would likely transact directly if the relationship was trusted and the direct price was cheaper.

Therefore:

- hiding contact details is not a sufficient retention strategy
- matching alone may be one-time value
- repeat use must be earned through transaction/admin/outcome value

Potential retention value to test:

- contract/NDA handling
- compliant safe payment
- tax/transaction evidence
- vendor history
- open-market comparison
- backup supply
- availability
- dispute support
- eventual SLA/outcome assurance

Monetization and platform-retention economics are **not validated**.

---

## 7. Existing-partner workflow finding

When a new task appears, the GM said they would probably contact existing vendors first and also use KAPAPI for comparison.

**Speed of the first BID was not the main issue in that ordinary comparison scenario.**

When offered a hypothetical KAPAPI workflow that:

- includes existing partners
- sends them a proposal request automatically
- exposes the same QUEST to new PLAYERs
- compares price / delivery / career / quality in one place

The GM preferred starting in KAPAPI and described the workflow as **meaningfully convenient**, though not yet indispensable.

### Important metric implication

Do not treat `time to first BID` as the single universal north-star metric.

Segment by context:

- **TIME ATTACK / deadline crisis:** response latency is critical
- **normal vendor comparison:** quality, comparison breadth, administration and availability may matter more

## 8. Strongest desired product outcome

The GM identified value in:

1. file upload → AI-generated QUEST/work instruction
2. recent price/delivery comparison from existing vendors
3. market reference price
4. availability of frequent vendors
5. contract/NDA/tax evidence automation
6. immediate replacement recommendation when a partner is unavailable
7. all outsourced work/vendor history in one place
8. bidding
9. ultimately: **throw the work in and receive the finished result; the GM only inspects**

This produced the strongest product insight so far:

> **The GM does not fundamentally want to find a PLAYER. The GM wants the right-quality result by the required time. If the process can be automated reliably, that is materially stronger than marketplace UX alone.**

## 9. Current GM hypotheses

### GM-01 — short capacity/deadline gaps recur

Small professional firms repeatedly encounter bursts where required work exceeds internal capacity.

### GM-02 — self-completion/overtime is a primary competitor

For easy tasks, search/explanation/management friction can exceed outsourcing benefit.

### GM-03 — delegation overhead can be reduced enough to change behavior

Clear SOW generation, career signals, standardized security and transaction administration can lower outsourcing friction.

### GM-04 — price + delivery time creates real selection value

Urgent GMs may rationally pay more for earlier completion when it protects higher-value work or avoids deadline failure.

### GM-05 — relevant career can matter more than generic ratings

Industry experience signals how much explanation/context transfer will be necessary.

### GM-06 — existing vendors do not eliminate market need

GMs still need new supply when availability, quality or specialty changes.

### GM-07 — KAPAPI can become an external-work operating layer

If existing partners and open-market PLAYERs are managed together, KAPAPI can enter the workflow before vendor selection.

### GM-08 — outcome orchestration can be stronger than marketplace discovery

If KAPAPI can reliably produce the required output on time with minimal GM management, the value proposition changes category.

### GM-09 — task categories have separate liquidity

PLAYER count across the whole platform does not guarantee liquidity for one QUEST type. CAD, video, development and translation should be measured as separate micro-markets.

### GM-10 — there is a viable task band between “AI/self-do it” and “large project”

KAPAPI assumes a meaningful market exists for work that is:

- too context-heavy or variable for full automation/self-service
- but small and clear enough to avoid large-project procurement overhead

This boundary is not yet proven and must be tested explicitly.

## 10. Supply/engine seed experiment

Founder-originated CAD work can solve part of the initial chicken-and-egg problem.

Working experiment discussed:

- ~10 real CAD QUESTs
- roughly KRW 300k–500k total founder-funded reward budget
- recruit qualified PLAYER candidates
- observe real BIDs and deliveries

Measure:

- qualified PLAYER signup/conversion
- valid BIDs per QUEST
- price distribution
- delivery-time distribution
- completion/on-time rate
- revisions
- willingness to BID again

### Rule

> **Founder-funded QUESTs validate supply and the transaction engine. They do not validate external GM demand.**

Do not mix them into market-demand KPIs.

## 11. Concierge GM experiment

Before full AI/automation exists, KAPAPI can manually perform the future product promise.

Test flow:

```text
external GM sends file + rough sentence
→ KAPAPI manually/AI-assistively creates SOW
→ PLAYERs BID
→ GM selects
→ work completes
```

Purpose:

- test whether near-zero posting friction changes behavior
- learn what information SOW generation actually requires
- observe where humans still need to intervene
- collect training/product requirements before automation

This is **people first, software second** market validation.

## 12. External GM acquisition / subsidy test

A limited first-QUEST subsidy may be tested as customer-acquisition spend.

Possible structure discussed earlier:

- small first-QUEST support for targeted professional firms
- measure first transaction and satisfaction
- most importantly, measure **self-funded repeat**

Subsidy amount and sample size are experiment variables, not committed policy.

### Strong signal

> platform support ends → GM spends own money on the second QUEST

### Weak signal

> GM happily consumes free work and disappears

A subsidized first transaction should never be reported as equivalent to organic paid demand.

## 13. SOW / explanation-friction experiment

Compare:

### A. Conventional posting

GM manually explains/scopes the work.

### B. Concierge/AI-assisted posting

GM uploads files + one short instruction; KAPAPI structures the SOW and asks only missing questions.

Measure:

- GM minutes to valid QUEST
- number of follow-up questions
- SOW corrections before posting
- PLAYER clarification requests
- revisions caused by scope ambiguity
- GM preference

This may be more important than sophisticated AI price prediction.

---

## 14. Quality architecture experiment

Do not initially test “Can AI judge professional quality?” as the core thesis.

Test a three-layer model:

1. objective automated preflight
2. GM acceptance/revision
3. platform dispute only when necessary

Measure:

- what percentage of deliverable checks can be objectively automated
- GM inspection time
- revision rate
- dispute rate
- staff minutes per disputed transaction

### Unit-economic risk

If a low-value transaction repeatedly requires substantial human dispute/inspection time, the marketplace may be economically unattractive even if users like it.

## 15. Monetization hypotheses

Monetization is open.

Potential models to test:

- PLAYER take rate
- GM transaction fee
- all-in price with platform economics embedded
- subscription/admin plan for frequent GMs
- hybrid models later

Do **not** assume a 10% take rate is validated.

Questions:

- does a fee accelerate direct-trade leakage?
- how much value do safe payment/NDA/evidence/backups create?
- can support/dispute cost fit inside low-ticket economics?
- does GM care more about total price than a separate fee line?

Early transaction-engine validation can use:

- KAPAPI fee = 0
- no KAPAPI custody of funds
- direct GM↔PLAYER payment

## 16. Tests to run before 2R

### Interview test

Target: 15–30 external GMs in one narrow segment.

Ask about real past behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm.

Record:

- last capacity/deadline gap
- actual fallback
- current external vendor network
- actual spend
- delegation barriers
- security concerns
- explanation burden
- vendor-selection criteria
- price vs deadline tradeoff

### Real QUEST test

Measure:

- QUEST/SOW creation time
- first valid BID time
- valid BIDs per QUEST
- chosen price/time/trust combination
- completion time
- revision count
- GM management minutes
- outcome acceptance

### External paid test

Founder-originated CAD tasks are mechanism tests, not demand proof.

Best evidence:

> external GM spends own money.

Stronger:

> same GM returns with another QUEST.

### Existing-partner test

Let existing vendors compete/be compared alongside open-market PLAYERs.

### Assist test

Compare normal QUEST creation against file + one sentence → generated SOW.

### TIME ATTACK test

Only in a true urgent scenario, measure:

- time to first qualified BID
- time to sufficient options
- successful on-time completion
- premium GM pays for earlier completion

Do not extrapolate TIME ATTACK behavior to normal work.

## 18. Go / Pivot / Kill framework

### GO signal

Repeated evidence that:

- real GMs encounter recurring capacity/deadline gaps
- they pay competent unknown/external PLAYERs under appropriate safeguards
- delegation is clearly easier than self-completion
- sufficient category-specific supply responds
- outcomes are accepted with manageable revisions
- meaningful self-funded repeat occurs

### PIVOT signal

Demand exists but one assumption fails, for example:

- only one task type has liquidity
- GM wants concierge/result guarantee but not visible bidding
- existing-partner management is stronger than open-market discovery
- certain price bands or segments produce workable economics while others do not

### KILL / major rethink signal

Repeated external tests show:

- GMs prefer overtime/self-completion even in real capacity crises
- unknown external professionals remain unacceptable despite safeguards
- explanation costs remain near self-work costs
- competent supply cannot serve at GM-acceptable prices
- category liquidity never becomes sufficient
- most target work is either AI-automated or too complex/project-like
- direct vendor relationships remove enough repeat value that acquisition economics fail
- dispute/quality support destroys unit economics

Do not protect the idea from negative evidence.
